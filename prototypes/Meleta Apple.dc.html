Transcript

00:00

Today we finish substitution reactions. Two mechanisms matter for the exam, and the difference between them is entirely about what the rate depends on.

04:18

In SN2 the nucleophile attacks the carbon while the leaving group is still attached, so the rate depends on both concentrations. Backside attack means inversion of configuration — that is the detail people lose marks on.

11:47

SN1 goes through a carbocation. The rate only depends on the substrate, tertiary centres are favoured, and polar protic solvents stabilise the intermediate.

16:02

Write the mechanism table out by hand once. Substrate, nucleophile strength, solvent, stereochemistry. If you can reproduce that table, the exam question is mechanical.

Show full transcript
